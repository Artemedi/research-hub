# ReA-Bench — responsible-agent audit with hard benign controls

**14.11.2026, full paper (50 pp) [arXiv 2611.16493v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16493v1), dated 12.11.2026, SHA-256 6ead6b710dc427d12afa848929e8e0cc3fda6d958e7a6367b7e37e6bfd8e43f3.** Read methods, trace adaptation, human/LLM QA, evaluations, and threats to validity. No upstream benchmark rerun, no DSH execution.

## Method

Authors collect de-identified trajectory seeds from production and red teaming, adapt them into six evaluation tiers: L1 policy/authorization knowledge, L2 evidence and contradictions, L3 long-context source location, L4 source and logical inference, L5 indirect steering, L6 missing external rules or runtime information. Construct difficult benign cases that resemble risky behavior but are authorized, with certain paired short/long or with/without-auxiliary-information versions. The benchmark contains model-generated and transformed traces rather than unmodified representative user traffic; human checks and automatic integrity checks are part of preparation. Methods include final answer, short chain-of-thought, full-history and windowed methods plus global-summary-assisted, with 8 model configurations. It tests auditing trajectories and risks, **not** general safety of the model.

## Quantified evidence and limits

At a threshold of 5, averages over four common methods: GPT-luna 87.0% risky recall / 52.0% false positives on benchmark benign controls; GPT-sol 88.5% / 54.0%; Grok-high 71.0% / 5.5%. These ratios depend on selected hard negatives, task mixture, calibration and one evaluation per model-method setting. A high score or a consensus verdict cannot establish that a side effect was safe. L6 materially depends on external authority snapshots and remote execution history, often not in captured trace. Audit method comparisons on partially different subsets are not an absolute ranking; samples were partly authored using AI and require future independent reproduction. False positives in production-like benign sample are lower than deliberately hard negatives, so no real-world incident rate inference.
